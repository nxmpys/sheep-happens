## Water has not changed

<Evidence kind="data">

**Conjure Water** costs the same, is learned at the same levels and its bottles last the same in both games. Up to rank 4 they also restore the same. <Status status="reported" />

<WaterRanks />

</Evidence>

<Evidence kind="data" title="The “20 bottles” are a ceiling">

The tooltip says 20, but the spell data gives 2 bottles when you learn the rank and 2 more per level. It takes 9 levels to reach 20. Rank 7 starts at 10, so at level 60 it stays at 10: Wowhead Forever shows 20 because it computes that tooltip at level 65. Classic and Forever share this formula. <Status status="reported" />

</Evidence>

## The mana pool does not grow as you would expect

<Evidence kind="data">

Several low level cloth pieces lose Intellect in exchange for spell power or mp5 (mana per 5 seconds). <Status status="reported" />

<WaterGear />

{/* TODO verificar: 15 mana per point of Intellect is the Classic figure; I could not confirm it in Forever. */}

The mana pool column assumes 15 per point of Intellect, as in Classic. <Status status="classic" />

There is new Intellect gear too: the Rotmender's Raiment set, from the Ruins of Lordaeron, gives +10 at 3 pieces and, at 4, returns 200 mana when you fall below 15%. Even so, the ForeverChanges best in slot list for Frost at level 30 puts Frost damage and spell power first. <Status status="reported" />

</Evidence>

<Evidence kind="inference" title="Less Intellect does not mean more bottles">

Bottles depend on the mana you spend, not on the mana you can hold: 3000 mana is the same number of bottles with a pool of 1500 or of 1000. A smaller pool makes you stop sooner and more often, for shorter breaks. That is why it feels so noticeable even when the total water is the same.

mp5 can even come out ahead: Mindthrust Bracers lose 60 from the pool and return it in 100 seconds, and you keep that mp5 while casting. In a long dungeon they give more mana than in Classic. Tree Bark Jacket is a different case: it loses 150 from the pool and returns nothing, it only gives you damage.

</Evidence>

<Evidence kind="inference" title="The full gear comparison is weak">

{/* TODO verificar: I could not check either Intellect total (61 and 89). */}

A level 19 Classic twink reaches 61 Intellect from gear alone. A level 20 Forever list reaches 89 in total, but that already includes a human's base and +5 from a staff enchant. Take those out and Forever comes out lower.

I only treat it as an order of magnitude. A twink is an extremely optimised character, the two lists are not the same level, and I could not check either total. The items in the table weigh more, because they are the same item in both games.

</Evidence>

## Spells do less per mana, at base

<Evidence kind="data">

All these spells cost the same mana in both games, so losing damage means losing damage per mana. <Status status="reported" />

<WaterDamage view="chart" />

<WaterDamage view="table" />

High ranks are the values at level 60. Wowhead Forever gives higher figures (Frostbolt 11: 470 to 506) because it computes its tooltips at level 64, which does not exist. On Blizzard rank 1, ForeverChanges gives 200 and Wowhead 196.

</Evidence>

<Evidence kind="inference" title="Base damage is not real damage">

The table compares the spell's damage without gear. The “SP to match” column is the spell power at which Forever catches a Classic character wearing none. It is a reference, not a real comparison: Classic also gains spell power at high levels.

What it does show is the difference between schools. **Frostbolt** rank 11 needs 74 spell power; **Fireball** rank 12, 195, and **Scorch** rank 7, 180. **Arcane Explosion** and **Blizzard** lose little, but spell power barely helps them: Arcane Explosion only uses 14.3%.

An example with Tree Bark Jacket, which gives +13 spell power instead of +10 Intellect:

On Frost, that piece almost closes the gap at low ranks. On Fire, less than half. ForeverChanges also measured on heals that a rank starts losing spell power 18 levels after you learn it, and assumes the same for damage.

</Evidence>

## More dungeons, more pulls

<Evidence kind="data">

I count a dungeon from the level its range starts, and each Scarlet Monastery wing on its own. The four new ones up to level 30 are Hall of Thanes, Ruins of Lordaeron, Excavation Site: Wetlands and City of Dalaran. <Status status="reported" />

<WaterDungeons view="chart" />

<WaterDungeons view="table" />

Experience is from quests, without kills, from the level 30 build of 1 October. The levels they give are worked out at the level each dungeon starts. Blizzard has cut experience from dungeon kills sharply and raised it for dungeon quests. <Status status="reported" />

</Evidence>

<Evidence kind="inference">

More dungeons existing is data; more dungeons being run is not. The experience split invites you to enter each one once for its quests rather than repeat the same one, so the real rise in pulls may be smaller than the table's. Still, a new level 15 dungeon worth 1.5 to 2 levels is hard to skip, and it did not exist before.

</Evidence>

## What offsets it

<Evidence kind="data">

Two sources of regeneration while casting go up a lot in Forever. **Arcane Meditation** gives 17% with a single point. <Status status="reported" />

<WaterCompensations />

Mana gems and **Evocation** do not change. Some items gain mp5 in exchange for Intellect, like those in the earlier table. I could not confirm whether Mage Armor and Arcane Meditation add up as in Classic. <Status status="pending" />

</Evidence>

<Evidence kind="inference" title="They help in combat, not in the break">

Regenerating while casting lowers the net mana a fight costs, so it does save some water. But it does not shorten the bottle or the break: once you sit down to drink you regenerate without those helpers. And they arrive late: there is no Mage Armor until level 34, and Arcane Meditation asks for 15 points in Arcane. Those are exactly the levels of the new dungeons in the table.

</Evidence>

## Mage water is group water

<Evidence kind="data">

Anyone who has the level in the “Needs level” column can use a batch, and it stacks to 20. Rank 7 comes from <PageLink to="class-quests">a class quest</PageLink>. <Status status="reported" />

</Evidence>

<Evidence kind="inference">

If your group spends more, you work more: the healer and the other casters drink what you conjure. I have not compared healers' mana in both games, so I do not know how much they drink now. ForeverChanges works out that, up to level 30, downranking pays them more than in Classic, which would save them water if they use it.

</Evidence>

## What to do in practice

<Evidence kind="advice" title="Allow a margin by school">

Without spell power, the same base damage takes up to 13% more mana with Frostbolt 11 and up to 40% more with Fireball 12. These are ceilings, not a forecast: the more spell power you wear, the less. If mana holds you back while leveling, Frost pays the least.

</Evidence>

<Evidence kind="advice" title="Five levels before each rank, buy water">

Each bottle can be drunk five levels before you learn to conjure it, and vendors already sell one with the same mana and the same time. During those levels, the bought one beats yours. <Status status="reported" />

| 45 to 49 | Morning Glory Dew | 2821 | 30 | 1915 |

</Evidence>

<Evidence kind="advice" title="Just after learning a rank, conjure the previous one for the group">

At level 20, rank 3 gives 2 bottles per cast and rank 2 gives 20. The new water restores more per second, but it takes a long time to fill your bags. The new rank only pays off a few levels later:

<WaterConjureBatches />

</Evidence>

<Evidence kind="advice" title="In a group, conjure before going in and watch the healer">

- Hand out water before the first pull and check they can drink it at their level.
- Before pulling, check their mana as well as yours. If they have to stop, drink too.
- A bottle is used up when you start drinking: if you stand up halfway, you lose the rest.
- Conjured items disappear if you stay logged out for more than 15 min: do not prepare them the night before. <Status status="reported" />

</Evidence>

<Evidence kind="advice" title="Save mana where it costs no damage">

- Finish with your <PageLink to="leveling">wand</PageLink>: it costs no mana and lets your mana recover.
- Against several enemies, Arcane Explosion and Blizzard have changed the least. See <PageLink to="aoe-farming">AoE farming</PageLink>.

</Evidence>

## Limits and what is not known

- **Base damage against real damage.** The percentages leave out gear, talents, crits and enemies, which may have changed too.
- **No measurements.** Nobody has published drinking times per pull in Forever and Classic. I do not have them either.
- **Gear.** Three items are examples, not a sample of the whole game.
- **Ranks 5 to 7.** Mana per bottle and bottles per cast still need checking.
- **Launch.** The beta is over and builds keep changing without Blizzard notes. I will review these figures when the game is out.
